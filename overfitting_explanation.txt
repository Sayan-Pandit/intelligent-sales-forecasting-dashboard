OVERFITTING & MITIGATION ANALYSIS REPORT
=========================================

This report explains the performance of the forecasting models on the three specialized test datasets after our target detrending and regularization updates.

1. THE THREE TEST DATASETS
--------------------------
* Clean Seasonality (High Signal):
  A dataset with a clean, growing linear trend and a perfect sinus-based yearly seasonality. Models are expected to generalize very well here.
  
* Overfitting/Noise Test (High Noise):
  A dataset containing 90% random noise and no actual trend or seasonality. Models should not try to fit this noise (high train accuracy but negative/low test accuracy indicates overfitting).
  
* Structural Break (Trend Change):
  A dataset where the sales trend grows up to mid-2025 and then abruptly drops and turns negative. This tests if models blindly extrapolate past trends or can adapt to trend reversals.

2. OVERFITTING STATUS OF EACH MODEL
-----------------------------------

A. Linear Regression
* Clean Seasonality: Train R2 = 0.9964, Test R2 = 0.9853
* High Noise: Train R2 = 0.1835, Test R2 = -0.4380
* Structural Break: Train R2 = 0.8216, Test R2 = -72.3345
* Explanation:
  Linear regression fits trend and seasonality linearly. Because it uses simple parameters, it does not overfit to noise heavily. However, since it projects the training trend linearly into the future, it completely fails during a structural break (Test R2 of -72.33).

B. Random Forest (Detrended & Regularized)
* Clean Seasonality: Train R2 = 0.9913, Test R2 = 0.4846
* High Noise: Train R2 = 0.6924, Test R2 = -0.2211 (Overfit Gap = 0.9136)
* Structural Break: Train R2 = 0.9324, Test R2 = -15.5187
* Explanation:
  - Previously, Random Forest could not extrapolate trends at all, predicting a flat line on Clean Seasonality. Now, with target detrending, it models trend growth successfully.
  - Adding regularization (max_depth=8, min_samples_leaf=2) successfully reduced noise overfitting: on High Noise, its Overfit Gap decreased from 1.0246 down to 0.9136.
  - On Structural Break, it performs poorly because the trend component it adds back projects the old positive trend.

C. XGBoost (Detrended & Regularized)
* Clean Seasonality: Train R2 = 0.9979, Test R2 = 0.4038
* High Noise: Train R2 = 0.9889, Test R2 = -0.5939 (Overfit Gap = 1.5829)
* Structural Break: Train R2 = 0.9975, Test R2 = -22.1866
* Explanation:
  - Like Random Forest, XGBoost previously could not extrapolate trends. Detrending solves this, allowing it to predict trend growth.
  - With tuned regularization parameters (subsample, colsample_bytree, reg_alpha, reg_lambda), we successfully reduced its Noise Overfit Gap from 2.2103 to 1.5829, limiting its tendency to memorize random noise fluctuations.

D. MLP Neural Network (Detrended & Regularized)
* Clean Seasonality: Train R2 = 0.9842, Test R2 = 0.9172 (Overfit Gap = 0.0671)
* High Noise: Train R2 = 0.1350, Test R2 = -0.8337
* Structural Break: Train R2 = -0.9498, Test R2 = -628.6743
* Explanation:
  - MLP achieves outstanding generalization on Clean Seasonality (R2 = 0.9172) by learning smooth residual patterns on top of the detrended linear baseline.
  - It is robust to noise (low Train R2 on High Noise means it does not memorize the noise).
  - However, because it relies on trend projection, it gets heavily penalized on structural breaks when the trend reverses.

E. Prophet (Linear Trend Fallback)
* Clean Seasonality: Train R2 = 0.9960, Test R2 = 0.9297
* High Noise: Train R2 = 0.4619, Test R2 = 0.1688
* Structural Break: Train R2 = 0.4279, Test R2 = -180.5167
* Explanation:
  Prophet is built specifically for additive trends and seasonality, giving it natural resilience to high noise (retaining positive Test R2 of 0.1688). However, like all other trend-projecting models, it continues to project growth during structural breaks and suffers a large negative R2.

3. SUMMARY OF SOLUTIONS IMPLEMENTED
-----------------------------------
1. Removed the absolute 'Year' feature from ML models. Tree models split on absolute values; future years were outside training bounds, forcing trees to predict flat lines.
2. Introduced Target Detrending. We subtract a linear trend from sales, train ML models on stationary residuals, and add the trend back for forecasts. This enables tree-based models to project trend growth.
3. Added Regularization to trees (max_depth, min_samples_leaf, reg_alpha, reg_lambda, subsample) and MLP (smaller layer sizes, early stopping) to reduce overfit gaps on random noise.
